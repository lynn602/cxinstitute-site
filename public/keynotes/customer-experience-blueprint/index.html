Customer Experience Blueprint — One Brand. One Connected Experience.

How service blueprinting connects the brand promise to how it's delivered.

Discover the connection between what customers experience and everything that must happen behind the scenes to deliver it consistently.

Book a discovery callEnquire about this keynote

One brand promise. Many moments that matter.

In this fascinating keynote, Lynn Baker explains the frontstage and backstage methodology that lies at the heart of service blueprinting. The frontstage is what customers see and experience. The backstage is the work that supports it, from how teams coordinate to the processes and technology they rely on.

One methodology. Four industries.

Explore how the customer-facing experience is supported by the people, processes and technology behind the scenes. Select an industry to see its frontstage and backstage.

Restaurants · Customer service
FRONTSTAGE

The guest experience

A welcoming arrival, attentive service, accurate orders and a smooth payment experience.

↕
BACKSTAGE

What makes it possible

Table allocation, kitchen handoffs, stock availability, staff scheduling and point-of-sale systems.

The blueprint connects both sides to reveal handoffs, dependencies and opportunities for improvement.

Frontstage — the customer experience

Every interaction customers can see: the welcome, the environment, the conversation, the transaction and the follow-through.

Backstage — how it's delivered

The people, processes and technology that support each customer touchpoint, including responsibilities shared between branches and head office.

From a promise to a practical blueprint

Using a live demonstration of TOUCHPOINTS Blueprint Studio, Lynn shows how franchisees and multi-location teams can connect these two layers to identify what their branches need to deliver the brand promise, plan who does what across head office and the branch, and put those service standards into practice.

Service example: a fast-food restaurant

Frontstage: A customer orders, pays, collects their meal and receives help if something is wrong.

Backstage: Staff coordination, kitchen preparation, stock availability, point-of-sale systems, order accuracy checks and service recovery procedures all determine whether the promise is delivered.

The blueprint makes dependencies visible so teams can improve the entire experience, not just the individual interaction.

What audiences take away

See the whole experience

Understand how every customer-facing moment depends on coordinated operational delivery.

Connect teams and standards

Clarify the roles of head office, franchisees, frontline teams and support functions.

Turn insight into action

Identify gaps, handoffs and opportunities for practical, measurable improvement.
